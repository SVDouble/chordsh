# Melody-first harmonization (design draft)

**Why melody first.** Chords mean little on their own. They are what turns melody notes into rest,
colour or pull ([harmony model §0](harmony-model.md#0-melody-first)). So chord.sh should start from the
melody you play and offer harmony as explanations of it: first a basic one, then more developed ones.

## Now: a chord assistant with independent judges

Automatic harmonization (the canvas mock) didn't feel right. For now, chord.sh only **assists**: for a
slot you're writing, it ranks chords and explains each one. Nothing is generated behind your back.

The ranking comes from separate **judges**. Each gives a verdict from 0 to 1, or none when it has
nothing to judge. You choose their weights, so you can hear and compare what each one says. The code is
in [src/engine/assist.ts](../src/engine/assist.ts).

| judge      | kind               | what it asks                                                                                         |
| ---------- | ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **melody** | rules              | Do the melody notes in the slot rest, colour or rub over the chord? It weights strong beats and long notes, and forgives passing and neighbour notes that resolve by step. |
| **rules**  | rules              | Is the motion from the previous chord and into the next one sensible? Resolve 1, glide (≥ 2 shared notes) 0.8, slide 0.6, open 0.6, step 0.5, stay 0.4, leap 0.2. Smooth movement counts 0.3. Leaving the key costs 0.1 unless the chord points somewhere (a V7 or °7). |
| **habit**  | data (fixed table) | How often do songs make these root moves? Uses the learned scale-degree transition table, which is repeatable but learned from data. |

**Default weights:** melody 0.5, rules 0.5, habit 0. Every suggestion also carries its reasons, e.g.
"melody rests on C, E · from C: glides to Am, keeping 2 notes · then resolves into G, brighter".

### How the judges compare on real melodies

Run with `npm run benchmark:assist` (options: `SPLIT=tune|test` and `CORPORA=…`).

**The test:** for every annotated chord, give the judges the melody over its span and the real chords on
either side. Then see where the real chord lands in their ranking. The melody is the top note of the
texture.

Test split, top-1 accuracy (top-5 in brackets):

| judges            | Beethoven     | Chopin        | Mozart        | POP909        |
| ----------------- | ------------- | ------------- | ------------- | ------------- |
| melody alone      | 55.6 (86.6)   | 44.5 (79.4)   | 50.8 (83.9)   | **43.8** (74.1) |
| rules alone       | 36.7 (79.4)   | 33.8 (72.9)   | 34.8 (78.2)   | 20.4 (48.5)   |
| habit alone       | 66.2 (83.0)   | 50.6 (76.7)   | 60.9 (84.2)   | 31.8 (61.5)   |
| melody + rules    | 56.4 (88.7)   | 50.9 (82.3)   | 53.1 (87.8)   | 38.3 (70.6)   |
| melody + habit    | **72.9** (90.2) | 61.7 (83.1) | **67.7** (90.5) | 41.6 (73.8) |
| all three         | 72.0 (90.1)   | **62.6** (83.6) | 67.1 (90.3) | 42.5 (75.2)   |

What the judges have to say:

- **The melody is the strongest single judge** in pop. In classical music, it is strong together with
  the neighbours.
- **The rules judge is the weakest.** When melody and rules disagree about the top chord, rules is right
  only 9–18% of the time; melody is right 33–44%. The rules favour falling fifths, while pop moves by
  loops and steps (I–V–vi–IV). The rules need to cover those moves, which is the next thing to improve.
- **Habit is strong in classical music but flattered by the test.** It sees the real chord on each side,
  and classical progressions are very regular. In pop it is clearly weaker than the melody.
- **When melody and rules agree, they are right 66–84% of the time.** That makes agreement a useful
  confidence signal to show in the interface.

**Caveats:**

- The melody is the top voice of a piano arrangement, which is noisy, especially in POP909.
- Telling the judges the real neighbouring chords is optimistic. Live, you only know what you've
  written so far.
- The jazz corpus is left out: its melodies were generated by our benchmark script.

## Four stages, entered from either side

Writers start in different places, so the tool supports both directions:

1. **Simple chords, possibly with no melody yet.** Sketch a basic progression by playing it, typing it,
   or picking one (I–V–vi–IV…).
2. **The melody**, written over those chords or on its own. The chords from stage 1 act as a guide:
   melody notes are coloured rest / colour / pull as you play.
3. **Simple chords filled in where they're missing.** If there is no progression yet, or it doesn't
   fit the melody in some bars, the basic harmonizer below proposes one, keeping any chords you fixed.
4. **Plenty of options to customize.** Alternatives for each slot, versions A/B/C, develop levels,
   happy ↔ sad, harmonic rhythm. Individual moves can be locked, swapped or undone, and everything stays
   editable in the timeline.

Stages 1 and 2 can repeat: change a chord and the melody's colours update; change the melody and the
bars that no longer fit are flagged with suggestions.

## The workflow, from a melody

1. **Record.** Play a melody from MIDI or the computer keyboard, or take a melody track from the
   timeline or a MIDI file.
2. **Find the bars.**
   - With a count-in or metronome, the bar grid comes straight from the tempo.
   - Without one, use `BeatTracker` + `decodeBars` (free tempo, as now).
   - Snap notes to the grid only lightly; the feel stays.
3. **Pick a harmonic rhythm.**
   - Default: one chord per bar.
   - Options: two per bar, or let the strong beats decide.
4. **Detect the key** from the melody, weighting notes by duration and metric position. Offer the top 3.
5. **Basic harmony.** Choose one chord per slot from the key's triads plus V7, using Viterbi (details
   below). Offer several distinct versions.
6. **Develop.** Elaborate the basic version step by step (details below). Every step is explained and
   must still fit the melody.
7. **Listen and compare.** The melody is shown coloured by its role over each chord. Each slot has
   ranked alternatives. Versions can be compared A/B, and everything is editable in the timeline.

## Basic harmony: scoring

**Fit:** how well chord _c_ explains a slot. Each melody note _n_ in the slot is weighted by its
duration × metric strength, then:

| role of the note over _c_ | score                                                                                     |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| rest                      | +1                                                                                        |
| colour                    | +0.3                                                                                      |
| lean or pull              | −1 on a strong beat. On a weak beat ~0, provided the next note resolves it by step (passing or neighbour note). |

**Transition** between chords:

- Scale-degree transitions learned from the corpora. `predictionModel` already holds these.
- Plus a cadence prior: end on I; V or IV before the end; phrase ends on bar 4 and 8.

**Output:** the k best paths, kept musically distinct (diverse beam). Three controls:

- **happy ↔ sad**: weights major versus minor readings, as in MySong;
- **expected ↔ fresh**: temperature on the transitions;
- **harmonic rhythm**: how often the chord changes.

## Develop: rewriting steps

Each move rewrites one part of the progression's tree. The terms come from Rohrmeier's grammar:
_preparation_ adds chords that lead into a goal, _prolongation_ stretches one chord across more of the
melody. A move is **accepted only if the melody still fits**: strong beats must stay rest or colour.

| move                  | example (in C)                                              | kind              |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| add implied colour    | melody holds B over C → Cmaj7; D over G7 → G9               | prolongation      |
| prepare a goal        | insert G7 before C; ii–V (Dm7 G7) before C                  | preparation       |
| secondary dominant    | A7 before Dm; E7 before Am                                  | preparation       |
| substitute            | tritone sub (D♭7 for G7); relative swap (Am for C); borrowed iv, ♭VI, ♭VII; backdoor (B♭7 → C) | substitution |
| passing chord         | C → C♯°7 → Dm; chromatic approach chord                     | passing           |
| pedal / ostinato      | keep C in the bass while the melody moves                   | prolongation      |

The development levels follow from how many moves you apply:

- **level 0**: basic;
- **level 1**: implied colour and V7s;
- **level 2**: ii–Vs and secondary dominants;
- **level 3**: substitutions, borrowed chords and passing chords.

This is the progression mutator and the goal tree working together, with the melody as the referee.

## What prior work tells us

- **[MySong](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/mysong/)** (Simon, Morris & Basu, CHI 2008):
  an HMM trained on songs picks chords for a sung melody. Users adjust them with sliders, including major
  ↔ minor. Its chords were rated about as highly as chords chosen by musicians. **Takeaway:** a plain HMM
  with easy-to-understand controls is a strong baseline.
- **[Yeh et al., "Automatic melody harmonization with triad chords: a comparative study"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02360)** (JNMR 2021):
  - Template matching and the HMM fit the melody well but were **conservative**, with little variety.
  - A genetic algorithm gave **variety but worse ratings**.
  - Deep models (BiLSTM, MTHarmonizer) covered more chords.
  - Listeners rated **fit above variety**.

  **Takeaway:** start safe, and make interest something the user asks for. That supports splitting the
  work into basic first and develop second.
- **[Sun et al., orderless NADE + chord balancing + blocked Gibbs sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13468)** (ICASSP 2021):
  - Class weights bring back chords that are reasonable but rare.
  - The number of Gibbs sampling passes trades coherence against interest.

  **Takeaway:** "develop" can be a dial, not just a switch.
- **Rohrmeier's grammar of jazz harmony; [Harasim, O'Donnell & Rohrmeier, ISMIR 2019](https://zenodo.org/records/3527812/files/ismir2019_paper_000039.pdf):**
  - Progressions are trees of preparation and prolongation.
  - A version that includes rhythm can generate chords together with where they fall in the bar.

  **Takeaway:** the "develop" moves above are tree rewrites, which makes them explainable.

## Data and evaluation

**A first test set of public-domain children's songs and folk tunes:** Twinkle Twinkle, Mary Had a
Little Lamb, Frère Jacques, Ode to Joy, London Bridge, Jingle Bells and Greensleeves (minor), plus a
"minor version" switch that flattens the 3rd and 6th. Their textbook harmonizations are well known
(Twinkle with two chords per bar: C C F C F C G C), so the basic harmonizer can be checked by ear and
by exact match. The design-canvas mock already passes Twinkle, Mary, Frère Jacques and Jingle Bells.

**Data with real melodies and chords:**

- **POP909** has a separate melody track. Our fetch script currently merges it into the other parts, so
  it must be kept separate.
- **Nottingham folk tunes** (melody plus chords, mostly public domain). Check the dataset licence first.
- **The Hooktheory-based set** used by Yeh et al., if we can get access.

**Metrics:**

- chord accuracy against the human chords (top-1 and top-3);
- melody fit: the share of melody time at rest or colour, as measured by the goal tree;
- chord variety: the CHE and CC measures from Yeh et al.;
- listening checks.

Same tune/test split and reproducible command as the [benchmark](benchmark.md).

## Milestones

1. **Offline prototype.** Implement melody → basic harmony with Viterbi and fit scoring. Measure it on
   POP909 (melody kept separate) and one folk corpus.
2. **Record a melody.** A melody lane in the timeline, with count-in and metronome.
3. **Basic harmony in the UI.** Alternatives for each slot, the three controls, and the melody coloured
   by role.
4. **Develop.** The rewriting moves, each with an explanation, the melody-fit check, and A/B listening.
5. **Evaluation.** Benchmark numbers plus listening sessions. Update [benchmark.md](benchmark.md) and
   [research.md](research.md).
