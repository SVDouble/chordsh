Which rules does the music follow?

Rules from music theory, grouped into four rulebooks, judged chord by chord against real music. A score of 50% means a rulebook can’t tell the composer’s chord from a random other chord in the same spot; 100% means it always prefers the real one. Trends over time are only drawn within one tradition, one dot per piece at its year.

Tricks to review

Frequent chord moves that functional harmony dislikes but composers keep using. Each comes with a name where theory has one, the rules that do support it (why it might work), the rules it breaks, and real examples to play. Mark them as you go; marks stay in this browser.

Keys and modes

Which pitch collection each piece uses inside its keys: scale degrees weighted by duration (melody plus chord tones, relative to the local key), matched to the smallest named collection that holds 95% of them. “Notes used” counts degrees with at least 3% of the weight.

Scales that change between octaves

Melodies that use one form of a degree low and the other high (for example ♭3 below, 3 above), so the scale does not repeat at the octave.

Constraints: how often are they honoured?

Of the chords a constraint concerns, the share that satisfy it: in the real music, and among random other chords placed in the same spot. The gap is what composers add.
